Demolition Derby Stunt Game Development Plan
Goal and scope
Build a single player browser stunt game in Three.js and TypeScript. The first arena has three selectable paths with increasing obstacle density, a tall classic arena monster truck, ramps and finish platforms. The core loop is choose a route, build speed, line up the jump, survive the hazards, land, and retry for a better result. Damage must change driving and create physical debris from the first playable build. Multiplayer, full soft body simulation, career progression and additional vehicles are later work.
Context and technical approach
The linked Research Agentic 3D Setup conversation recommends Three.js for easy browser iteration, Rapier for vehicle physics, authored detachable parts and localized dents, Poly Haven PBR textures, and repeatable visual and gameplay validation. We will follow that architecture. High polygon rendering and simple collision geometry remain separate. Use one local coding agent, deterministic asset scripts and saved test scenarios to control iteration cost.
Damage architecture
Represent the monster truck as a named assembly: front and rear chassis sections, four wheels, hood, roof, two doors, bed and two bumpers. Each part has health, mass, attachment, local transform and damage response. Detached pieces become dynamic rigid bodies with inherited motion. Losing wheels removes their traction and suspension; severing the chassis releases the rear assembly and disables propulsion. Collision energy and explicit lab tools use the same damage path. Deform body mesh vertices locally around an impact, cap displacement and rebuild normals. This is bounded cosmetic crumpling with rigid body breakage, not a BeamNG style soft body solver.
Milestone 1 Reproducible foundation
Chunk 1: establish Vite, TypeScript, Three.js, Rapier, Vitest and Playwright with locked dependencies, build checks and CI. Write failing fixed step clock tests before its implementation. Acceptance: one second at regular and accelerated playback runs the same number of physics ticks; invalid rates are rejected. Record the plan locally after this Google Doc exists. Create master from the existing main branch and push every passing chunk to master without rewriting main.
Milestone 2 Driving and jump physics
Chunk 2: implement a renderer independent fixed timestep world, raycast suspension, throttle, steering, braking and reset. Tests verify resting stability, acceleration, braking, steering, reset isolation and repeatability. Chunk 3: define three routes as shared collision and render data with launch ramps, gaps, landing platforms and finish triggers. Write integration tests that drive from rest with normal throttle through each entire route, prove airborne travel and stable landing at the finish, and demonstrate that low speed or incorrect alignment fails. No test may teleport the truck to the finish or use a different physics implementation.
Milestone 3 Destruction as gameplay
Chunk 4: implement per part health, localized deformation, physical detached debris, wheel failure and chassis severing. Start with failing tests for each damage transition, attachment cascade, repeated destruction, inherited velocity and handling changes. Chunk 5: add a destruction lab with a part selector, damage tool, chassis cutter, reset and readouts. Tests isolate every destructible part on a fresh vehicle, inspect its actual physical and visible state and confirm reset restores the whole assembly. Verify ordinary obstacle impacts also reach this system.
Milestone 4 First playable and test harness
Chunk 6: render the arena, truck, chase camera, route selection, speed and damage HUD, restart and result states. The first playable is gated on the browser harness already being available. Expose a development test bridge for inputs, deterministic stepping, telemetry, route reset and lab actions. Browser tests operate the real page and physics, at 1x and 16x playback, compare outcomes and capture screenshots, console errors and telemetry on failure. Automated playthroughs must finish all three routes; manual browser input must also be checked.
Milestone 5 Hazards and art
Chunk 7: add flames with exposure damage, rotating physical hammers, spike pits and an emissive lava pit, with increasing counts on easy, medium and hard paths. Test contact, escape, repeat damage limits and finite state after high speed impacts. Keep feasible launch windows and test successful runs with the hazards enabled. Chunk 8: author a reusable detailed monster truck with rounded body panels, large treaded tires, visible axles, suspension, roll cage, lights and livery. Aim for roughly 60,000 to 120,000 visible triangles for the hero truck while keeping collision geometry simple. Validate mesh detail, named parts, pivot placement and deformation support. Review a turntable, intact and broken states, and gameplay screenshots.
Materials and performance
Use locally cached Poly Haven road, grass and weathered metal PBR maps, including normal and roughness maps where available. Record source URLs, CC0 license, exact downloaded files and hashes in an asset manifest. Use metal maps on spikes and hazard machinery. Lava and animated flames need emissive and animated effects rather than treating a static surface texture as fire; combine these with Poly Haven ground and rock materials. Start with 1K maps, repeat UVs at physical scales and avoid runtime dependence on a remote texture service. Audit requests for missing textures. Profile the detailed truck, shadows, debris and effects in browser; cap debris and reuse shared geometry and materials. Target 60 fps on a typical desktop as a measured optimization goal, with a lower quality option if needed.
Test and commit protocol
For each chunk: write a behavior test, run it and record the expected failure, implement the smallest coherent solution, rerun the targeted tests, then run typecheck, production build and relevant existing regression tests. Commit the complete passing chunk and push to origin master. Keep a short development log with test commands, red and green evidence, commit purpose and limitations. Never push a known failing chunk. Browser tests check real rigid body movement, actual finish contacts, all parts including each wheel and the rear chassis, visual detachment and cleanup, and both 1x and 16x rates. Accelerated playback increases fixed ticks per rendered frame; it never enlarges the physics timestep.
Release gate and later milestones
A basic playable release requires all three routes to finish through normal driving in the browser harness, negative jump cases, a working destruction lab, independent destruction of every part, a visible detailed truck, local Poly Haven materials, no page errors, a clean production build, documented controls and reproducible setup. Capture evidence and report actual results rather than claiming artistic quality or performance solely from automated tests. Later milestones add authored GLB assets from Blender, stronger suspension tuning, audio, richer deformation, replay ghosts, scoring progression and additional arenas. Full soft body destruction requires a separate feasibility study.
